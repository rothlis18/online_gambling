### **2. Structural Resilience Threshold of Localized Edge Networks**

**Model:**  
Localized, air-gapped edge networks operate with **open weights** (natively in RAM) under **network scarcity** or **corporate access blockades**. Resilience is determined by **data retention capacity** and **compute redundancy**.

- **Resilience Threshold Equation:**  
  $$
  R_{\text{threshold}} = \frac{S_{\text{RAM}} \cdot \Delta t}{D_{\text{max}}}
  $$  
  Where:  
  - $ R_{\text{threshold}} $ = time until data loss (s)  
  - $ S_{\text{RAM}} $ = RAM size (GB)  
  - $ \Delta t $ = time interval between data offloads (s)  
  - $ D_{\text{max}} $ = maximum data rate (GB/s)  

**Example:**  
A 16 GB RAM system with $ \Delta t = 3600 $ s (1 hour) and $ D_{\text{max}} = 0.5 $ GB/s can sustain operations for:  
$$
R_{\text{threshold}} = \frac{16 \cdot 3600}{0.5} = 115,200 \text{ s} \approx 32 \text{ hours}
$$  
Under a 72-hour blockade, this system would fail unless **data is pre-stored** in non-volatile memory.

- **Transaction Rate Equation:**  
  $$

  Where:  
  - $ Q_{\text{max}} $ = maximum queries per second (QPS)

  - $ \mu_{\text{query}} $ = query cost per token (tokens/QPS)  
  - $ C_{\text{token}} $ = token value (e.g., $1/token)  

**Example:**  
A system with $ T_{\text{token}} = 10^6 $ tokens, $ \mu_{\text{query}} = 0.01 $ tokens/QPS, and $ C_{\text{token}} = 1 $ can sustain:  
$$
Q_{\text{max}} = \frac{10^6 \cdot 0.01}{1} = 10,000 \text{ QPS}
$$

### **4. VRAM/Compute Constraints for Self-Sustaining Offline Data Fortresses**

**Operational Perimeter:**  
A data fortress requires **VRAM**, **compute power**, and **energy efficiency** to sustain operations offline.

- **VRAM Requirement:**  
  $$
  V_{\text{RAM}} = \frac{D_{\text{storage}} \cdot \log_2(N)}{f_{\text{compression}}}
  $$  
  Where:  
  - $ D_{\text{storage}} $ = data storage capacity (GB)  
  - $ N $ = number of data points  
  - $ f_{\text{compression}} $ = compression ratio (e.g., 10x)  

- **Compute Power:**  
  $$
  C_{\text{compute}} = \frac{D_{\text{processing}} \cdot \log_2(\epsilon)}{t_{\text{latency}}}
  $$  
  Where:  
  - $ D_{\text{processing}} $ = data processing rate (GB/s)  
  - $ \epsilon $ = error tolerance (e.g., 1e-6)  
  - $ t_{\text{latency}} $ = acceptable latency (s)  

**Example:**  
A fortress storing 1 PB (10^15 B) of data with $ f_{\text{compression}} = 10 $, $ \epsilon = 1e-6 $, and $ t_{\text{latency}} = 0.1 $ s requires:  
$$
V_{\text{RAM}} = \frac{10^{15} \cdot \log_2(10^6)}{10} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{12} \text{ B} = 1.5 \text{ TB}
$$  
$$
C_{\text{compute}} = \frac{10^{15} \cdot \log_2(1e-6)}{0.1} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ FLOPS}
$$  

**Energy Efficiency:**  
$$
E_{\text{total}} = \frac{P_{\text{compute}} \cdot t_{\text{operation}}}{\eta_{\text{efficiency}}}
$$  
Where:  
- $ P_{\text{compute}} $ = power consumption (W)
